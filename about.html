ABOUT

Lauren Housego is a multi-disciplinary visual artist working across animation and illustration. Her animation practice explores the social and power structures of capitalism through stop-motion and puppets that embody the consequences of living within this system. She explores notions of social pressure, bureaucracy, conformity, the absence of connection and, more recently, ideas of work, productivity and exhaustion. She is often inspired by absurdist literature which questions the roles we play and highlights the nonsensical in our behaviour as a society. Her animations employ characteristics typical of absurdism: characters caught in hopeless situations forced to do meaningless actions as if they are automatons, comedy mixed with tragedy and the disintegration of language which symbolizes failed communication between characters.

Her animations have been exhibited nationally and internationally, including in venues such as Picturehouse Central, London and the Australian Design Center in Sydney as part of the Craft Council UK’s Real to Reel festival, Oriel Davies Gallery and Gesticulation festival in Switzerland. She taught for six years in São Paulo, Brazil on the BA Illustration at the British School of Creative Arts (EBAC). She currently resides in Brighton, UK, and is a lecturer on the BA (Hons) Animation.
